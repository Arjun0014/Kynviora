Kynviora V3 · design language

Change is the thing this app is best at showing

V3 keeps the V2 structure and rebuilds four things: the agent becomes a persistent bar rather than a bubble, Health becomes a medical record rather than a metrics dashboard, Care becomes a household rather than a settings page, and You becomes an addressable hub. Under all four sits one new idea — a visual language for changed since last time, kept separate from danger.

01

Four kinds of colour

V2 had two: decorative tint and semantic state. V3 names interface colour, and splits change out of the semantic set. Change used to be drawn as informational blue, which made a new lab value look like a footnote, or as attention amber, which made it look like a problem. It is neither.

1 · Decorative Category and avatar tints. Chroma at or below 0.06, hues held away from every semantic hue. A tint says "shampoo" or "Anita"; it never says safe.
2 · Interface Listening One hue for everything the person drives: selected tab, chosen filter, the talk bar, a running job, a scrubbed chart point. Teal is the app answering a touch.
3 · Change · new in V3 ◆ ChangedNEW▲ +1.2 Blue-violet, 7.4:1 on the dark canvas and 8.1:1 on light. Marks a difference between two records: a new report, a moved value, a changed formula, an altered grant. Not red, because a change is not a danger. Not blue, because it is not a footnote.
4 · State ○ Nothing recorded ◇ Imported once ◐ Needs input ▲ Reviewed notice ● Completed ▨ Stale Each state keeps a glyph and a word, so theme, tint and colour vision are all irrelevant to meaning. Positive marks a completed operation, never a product.
Surface grading replaces flat greyA card that needs something now carries a short attention wash from its top edge; a card about change carries a violet one; a running job carries teal. Each gradient fades to the ordinary surface within 70–80% of the card, so density never becomes noise, and the tint always arrives with that tone's border and label. Ordinary content stays neutral — which is what makes the graded cards readable at a glance.
02

Talk to Kynviora · a bar, eight states, no thread

Where it livesFull width, immediately above the bottom navigation on the five destinations and above the safe area on detail screens. It leaves only for camera capture, an open sheet and selection mode, where the space belongs to the task. Idle it is a quiet surface with a teal outline and a still waveform; live it takes the selection tint, animates, and gains a stop button.
Eight states, each a wordTalk to Kynviora · Listening · Understanding · Working · Needs your confirmation · Done · Could not do that · Needs more information. The bar's label is the state, so nothing rests on the animation, and the sub-line says what happens next rather than what is happening now.
The screen is the answerA panel rises above the bar carrying only what the screen cannot: the transcript, phrasings for this route, a spoken result, a confirmation. The result lands in the UI — the range changes, the filter applies, the report opens. Nothing accumulates in a thread, so there is no history to scroll and no illusion of a conversation partner.
Refusal is a designed stateAsk it to delete the account and it says where the control is and that it cannot operate it. Revoking access, exporting, consent and deletion are touch-only with a fresh sign-in; the agent's job there is navigation, not action. The panel footer always shows the exact context snapshot that left the phone — route, profile, what is focused, what is selected — because that is the whole of what was sent.
03

Health · four layers, one person

V2's Health was a wearable dashboard with a domain grid. V3 makes it a record with four addressable layers, reachable by segment or by voice: Profile, Records, Trends, History. Wearable data is one layer of four, not the subject.

ProfileA health passport: person, date of birth only because it is recorded, connected sources as chips, sync time. Then the change block, then allergies, conditions, medicines and matched sensitivities as four tappable counts, then latest measurements as small rich cards, then recent records. No grade, no ring, no composite.
RecordsNot a file browser. Each record is a structured summary card — kind, provider, date, count of structured results, changed count, source-flagged count, what the original document is, and how it was extracted. Filtering to Conditions or Allergies says plainly that those are recorded facts rather than documents, and points to where they live.
TrendsGrouped by domain, each row a sparkline, a last value and its source. Tapping opens a detail sized to the data: paired bars for blood pressure, a stage timeline for sleep, area and line for heart metrics, sparse dashed points for labs.
HistoryOne longitudinal timeline across records, labs, medicines, measurements and conditions, filterable by kind. Entries that represent a change carry the violet marker and tint; the rest stay neutral. Most entries open the thing they describe.
Why no glowing bodyAn anatomical map was drawn and rejected. Tinting an organ implies a judgement about that organ, and Kynviora has lab values, a few measurements and some typed-in facts — nothing that licenses colouring a kidney. The domain grid says the same thing honestly: which domains hold data, which changed, where the gaps are. The counts are the navigation.

Labs · the flagship, and the strictest screen in the app

Reference Band Row
TSHREF 0.4 – 4.0 mIU/L5.2▲ +0.4 vs 4.8 ▲ Flagged High by source report
The band is the interval the report gave; the thin tick is the previous comparable value; the thick mark is this one. A value sitting outside the band is a fact about the report, so it can be drawn. The amber chip appears only when the report itself flagged the result, and says so in those words. Kynviora never decides that a number is normal.
Sparse Longitudinal Chart Five readings across three years. The connecting line is dashed on purpose: a solid line between points months apart would claim a trajectory nobody measured. The shaded band is the report's interval. Wearable trends get solid lines because they are sampled continuously; labs never do.
Change Lens
◆ Changed · 1 4.8→5.2▲ +0.4
One reusable pattern answering six questions: what changed, against what, when, from which source, whether anything is asked of you, and what is unknown. Sections are Changed, New, Not repeated and Similar — the last collapsed. It runs the lab comparison, the annual checkup, formula changes, medicine records and access changes. "Changed" is defined, stated on screen, and computed: the value moved by more than a tenth of the previous one for year-on-year comparisons, or a twentieth for a short-interval repeat. Every count on every screen is derived from the same rule, so no two surfaces can disagree.

Counts first, then Changed, New, Not repeated, then seven collapsible provider panels. The counts describe the difference between two documents and say so. A test that was not repeated is shown with last year's value and the plain admission that Kynviora does not know why — an absence is not a result.
05

Trends · the visual matches the data

Blood pressureEach reading is one vertical bar from diastolic to systolic, with both series drawn over it — solid and dashed, not two colours. A pair of numbers taken together should look like a pair.
SleepSeven nights as stage bars, with bedtime and wake range, then duration over time below. Stages appear only because the watch reported them; if it did not, the bar is a single duration.
Gaps stay gapsFour days the watch was not worn. The line breaks, the span is hatched, and the legend says what happened. Interpolating would invent four measurements.
Scrub, and one comparison7D / 30D / 3M / 6M / 1Y, a crosshair on the selected point, and a detail card giving value, date, window, device and how the device produced it. One dashed reference line shows the average of the earlier half of the period — the only comparison offered, because a second baseline invites a conclusion. Stats name entries and gaps.

Care · a household, and You · an address for everything

Care Circle card
AR Anita RaoDAUGHTEREveryday caregiverSafety · Shelf · Medicines · CareRecord doses · Manage medicines● ActiveUntil 14 Dec
A person, a relationship, a role in the household, then the two grant lines drawn with the same solid/dashed marks the permission screens use, then status and expiry as chips, then their last action with a timestamp. Four visual statuses: Active, Invitation pending, Expires in n days, No expiry set. The matrix moves one level down, and the editor another — so the first screen is people, not policy.
Permission editor, and responsibilities Nine rows, one grant each, labelled with which of the three kinds it is and whether it is granted. Owner-only rows are visibly unavailable rather than hidden. A switch does not move until the server confirms, and the copy says so — an optimistic toggle on an access control is a lie about who can see a medicine. Below the circle, a light responsibilities layer records who tends to do what — doses, supply checks, appointment preparation, alerts. It grants nothing and claims no clinical authority; households forget, and this is a note, not a role. Care activity is a filterable timeline where looking is recorded alongside changing, because in a shared household both matter. Access events carry the change tint.
You · eight groups Account Center · Privacy & Data · Notifications & Reminders · Accessibility · Appearance · Connections · Kynviora Agent · Help & Support. The home screen is a profile header and eight rows with an icon, a name and a one-line description of what is inside. No raw switches, no destructive action, nothing needing a decision. Closing the account moved into Account Center, at the bottom of its own action-toned group, after identity, security and devices. Deep enough to be deliberate, discoverable enough to be findable, and explicit about the thirty-day window and what happens to a caregiver's records. Taking a copy of your data sits in Privacy & Data. Both need a fresh sign-in and neither is reachable by voice.
Connections · six honest states ● CONNECTED◐ BY HAND◇ IMPORTED ONCE▨ STALE○ NOT CONNECTED A document imported once is not a live connection. A scale with no entry in three weeks is stale, not agreeing. Apple Health is listed as designed and not implemented rather than shown as an inviting empty state. Nothing here pretends to be connected when it is not.
07

What V3 keeps, and what it still refuses

Untouched from V2Shelf and Considering, image-led tiles and category grouping, the Safety Lens, the selection tray, the infographic comparison, product detail with its four tabs, the Coverage Center, Agent Jobs and Activity, guided capture, Elder Mode. Today keeps its architecture and only changes order: needs you, reports, jobs, since you last looked, recent activity — with agent work always attributable to who asked and when.
Still refusedNo health score, safety score or composite. No diagnosis, and no cause inferred from a wearable change. No medication recommendation, no "best medicine", no auto-start or auto-stop. No all-clear state — a missing match is not approval. No colour-only meaning. No value called medically normal or abnormal unless the source report said so or an approved deterministic rule allows it. Evidence and urgency stay in different geometry and never sit adjacent.
MotionSheets and the talk panel rise in 320ms. The only continuous animations are the listening waveform, the listening pulse and a running job's ring — each reporting a live state. A changed value does not animate into place; a chart point moves without transition. Reduce motion collapses all of it to zero. Nothing animates a safety sentence into view.
Still to designOnboarding and authentication. The invitation flow in full. The review-task editor. Imaging reports and procedures as record kinds. The personal-relevance layer on Shelf, which needs the deterministic rule set before it can be drawn. Every loading, offline, error and permission-removed state per route. And real pack photographs and provider documents in place of the drawn silhouettes.
